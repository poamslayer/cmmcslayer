## The finding that mattered

The key listed 3.1.1 as NOT MET on objectives [a], [c], and [f]. The tester reported all six and showed its work.

It was right, and the key was under-specified when it was written. Objective [b] asks that processes acting on behalf of users are identified: the engagement's only evidence lists two provider service accounts as ordinary rows, with nothing naming them as non-person accounts, saying what they do, or recording how they were approved. Objective [e] asks that access is limited to those processes: both accounts hold standing Global Administrator with no restriction. Objective [d] asks that access is limited to authorized users, and the control file's own Insufficient bullets tag [d] for approval with no record kept and for the claim that staff informally know who should have access, which is exactly what the engagement's procedure describes.

The key was corrected rather than the tester marked wrong. The requirement-level finding and the point value are unchanged, so the SPRS score of 90 and the POA&M eligibility split still hold.

## What the audit did well, worth keeping

**It caught the evidence arguing against itself.** The submitted export states in its own text that it shows who has an account rather than who was authorized to have one. The audit quoted that back as the gap, which is the strongest possible form of the finding.

**It refused to lean on files it could not trust.** It noted that the asset inventory is an uncorrected draft and that the responsibility matrix is a stub, and said which questions it therefore could not settle, rather than treating a placeholder as an answer.

**It named gaps, not fixes**, as the skill requires, leaving the remedy to `remediate`.

## 3.5.3, the adjustable requirement

Also audited, also NOT MET, on all four objectives. The useful part was what it could not do: the control file carries `points: "3 or 5"` because the deduction depends on how far the implementation got, and the output template asked for a single number. The tester reported both and left the adjustment uncalculated rather than guess, which was the right call and a gap in the skill.

## Four gaps the tester reported, all fixed

**Policies were scoped too narrowly.** The skill gave the subagent only the `policies/` files the SSP names. Neither SSP file names any, so the procedure that contradicts the SSP would have been out of view. A contradiction between a statement and a policy the statement never mentions is exactly the kind an assessor finds. The subagent now gets the whole policy set.

**The environment was not an allowed input.** The skill listed only the engagement's own scope files, and the tester pointed out that an auditor following it literally would have no ground truth, only the engagement's uncorrected assertions. First-hand description of the environment is now an input, and where it disagrees with the engagement's scope files the environment wins and the disagreement is itself a finding.

**Adjustable requirements had no rendering rule.** The Points field now takes the value the current state earns with the state in brackets, because that distinction is what decides whether 3.13.11 can sit on a POA&M at all, and an audit that leaves it as a range hands the next skill a question it cannot answer.

**CONTESTED had no threshold.** The tester derived one and applied it: an edge case describing an arrangement this engagement does not have leaves nothing to contest, so the objective is simply NOT MET. The skill now states that, because marking every objective whose control file mentions a disagreement as CONTESTED turns the report into mush.
