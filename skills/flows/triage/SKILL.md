# Triage

A document arrives full of findings somebody else wrote. A mock assessment report, a C3PAO readiness review, a prime's supplier questionnaire, an insurer's checklist, a previous consultant's gap analysis. It is prose, it mixes real gaps with opinions, it cites no assessment objectives, and some of it is wrong.

Triage turns that into tickets the engagement can actually work.

**Only for findings the operator did not create.** Tickets that `/to-poam` produced are already agent-ready by construction, because they came from an audit against the objectives. Do not run them through this.

The tracker and the label vocabulary should have been provided to you. If `docs/agents/triage-labels.md` is missing, tell the operator to run `/setup-poamslayer-skills`.

Every comment or ticket this skill posts starts with:

```
> *Generated by an agent during triage.*
```

## The labels

Two category labels:

- `poam` or `opa` once the finding is confirmed and routed, applied by `/to-poam` rather than here.
- `req:<id>` for the requirement the finding bears on, once you know it.

Five state labels, and every triaged finding carries exactly one:

- `needs-triage`: not yet evaluated.
- `needs-info`: waiting on somebody, usually the person who wrote the finding or someone inside the company.
- `ready-for-agent`: confirmed, mapped to objectives, and specified well enough to work without further judgment.
- `ready-for-human`: confirmed, but closing it needs a decision, an external party, money, or a hand on hardware.
- `wontfix`: not going to be actioned, with the reason recorded.

## Method

### 1. Split the document into findings

One finding per claim. A paragraph that says three things is three findings. Number them and keep the author's own wording next to your restatement, because the operator may have to go back to whoever wrote it.

### 2. Map each finding to requirements and objectives

This is the step that makes the rest possible. An outside finding says "shared login on the shop floor". The ticket has to say which objectives that bears on. Call the Skill tool with `control-evidence-lookup` for each candidate requirement to check what the objective actually asks for.

A finding that maps to no objective is not necessarily wrong, but it is not a compliance gap. It goes to `wontfix` with the reason, or to the operator as a business risk worth noting outside the tracker.

### 3. Verify before you believe it

Do not open a ticket on someone else's say-so. Check the claim against the engagement repo and the environment:

- **Already fixed?** Check `evidence/<id>/` and the SSP. A report written in the spring describes the spring.
- **Already known?** Check the tracker for an open ticket on the same requirement, and `.scratch/audit/` for an audit that already found or cleared it. Link rather than duplicate.
- **Actually true?** Where you can check it directly, check it. A verified finding makes a far stronger ticket than a repeated one.
- **Wrong?** Reviewers get things wrong, most often by grading something they did not look at. Say so plainly and close it with the evidence.

### 4. Check it against the bar before accepting the recommendation

An outside finding usually arrives with a recommended fix attached, and the recommendation is the least reliable part. Separate the **gap** from the **proposed remedy**, and run the remedy past `over-engineering-guard` by calling the Skill tool. A real gap with an oversized remedy becomes a ticket for the gap, with a note that the recommendation goes past what any objective requires and the cheaper accepted thing that closes it.

A finding that is *only* a recommendation, with no gap under it, is `wontfix` with the guard's reasoning.

### 5. Grill what is too vague to work

If a finding is real but unspecific, call the Skill tool twice, for `grilling` and `domain-modeling`, and sharpen it a round of questions at a time. Update `CONTEXT.md` as terms land. If what is missing lives in someone else's head, use `to-questionnaire` and set the finding to `needs-info`.

### 6. Apply the outcome

- **`ready-for-agent`**: post the brief below.
- **`ready-for-human`**: the same brief, plus why it cannot be delegated: a decision, a purchase, a third party, physical access.
- **`needs-info`**: post what is established so far and the specific questions outstanding, naming who they are for. Never "please provide more information".
- **`wontfix`**: close with the reason, which is one of already implemented, not a compliance gap, factually wrong, or over-engineering. Keep the reason, because the same finding comes back next year from a different reviewer.

### 7. Hand off

Confirmed gaps that need an audit go to `/audit` for the requirement, which produces the objective-level finding, and then to `/to-poam`, which routes and gates it. Triage does not decide POA&M eligibility and does not set a closeout date.

## Ticket brief

```markdown
> *Generated by an agent during triage.*

## Finding

<the gap in one sentence, in this repo's vocabulary, not the reviewer's>

## Source

<the document, its date, and the author's own wording of the finding>

## Requirement and objectives

<id> <title>: objectives <letters>, and what each asks for.

## Verification

<what you checked and what you found: confirmed against the environment, confirmed against the repo, or not yet verifiable and why>

## Desired state

<what has to be true for the objectives to be MET. Not a solution design.>

## Out of scope

<anything adjacent this ticket is not>

## Done when

`/audit <id>` reports objectives <letters> MET.
```

Describe behaviour, not implementation. Do not reference line numbers in the source document or paths that will move. The brief has to still make sense in three months, because a finding can sit in `needs-info` for a quarter waiting on a prime.

## Seat notes

**OSC seat**: findings usually arrive from a consultant or a prime and land on you. The most valuable output is the `wontfix` pile with reasons, because it is what stops you spending money on somebody's boilerplate.

**Consultant seat**: when the document is another consultant's work, keep their wording next to yours in the Source section. You may have to explain to the client why you disagree, and quoting them accurately is what makes that conversation short.
